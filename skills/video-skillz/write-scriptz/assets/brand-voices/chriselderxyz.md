# Brand Voice
## Who's talking

Chris Elder. Full stack dev, 6.5 years into recovering from a concussion and neck injury from a mountain bike crash. 

The disability is context, never a pity play. It's used to explain constraints ("with the limited screen capacity from post concussion"), to earn authority on struggle, and to open the door to others: *"if you're dealing with concussion or neck issues too, DM's are always open."*

## Structural signatures
### The Sign Off
> "As always, keep breathing, keep building, and I'll see ya next time, peace out!"
### Cold Opens
#### With Temperature
Cold Tub videos state the water temp in the first three lines, often with a bit:

> "And the duck says were sitting at… **temperature**"
> "Annnd we're sitting at **Temperature**, very cozy."
> "We're sittin at… **temp** Fahrenheit, or **temp** Celcius."
#### Hook First, Intro Second
The thesis is line one. No preamble, no "in today's video". The self-intro is *delayed* until after the hook has landed:

> "Keep your code DRY is the worst advice I can imagine giving a new programmer."
> *(…four lines of argument…)*
> "Hi, I'm Chris, and this is Cold Tub Tech!"

#### Other Examples
- *"Recursion is never faster than regular loop iteration."*
- *"Stop. Putting. Everything. In. A Goroutine."*
- *"Go has wayyyyy too many ways to create a variable."*
### Stage Directions in Bold

for anything non-verbal — most common are `**Run Code**`, `**Highlight**`, `**Next**`, `**Show Diagram**`, plus performance beats: `**Breath out**`, `**Get In**`, `**Drum Roll**`, `**lol**`, `**Wave**`.
### One Line Per Breath
Lines break where the voice pauses, and end on commas rather than periods. The line break *is* the punctuation:

> "So instead of Don't Repeat Yourself,
> The BETTER Rule that I like to follow,
> is Don't Repeat Yourself More Than 3 times"
### Call To Actions
Mid-roll CTAs ride inside the content, never as a hard break:
> "And if you want to see more deep dives like this, theeenn.. subscribe for more!"
> "There are also some more gotchas in composition that I'll cover in the next video… *like and subscribe* (whispered)"

## Purposefully incorrect grammar and typography

This is the most distinctive layer. The script is a **performance score**, not prose — spelling is bent to encode delivery.

### Vowel Elongation = Hold the Syllable
> wayyyy · soooo · neeeed · Reeeeasonable · YEARSSS · whhhhole · verrrry · tooooo · Nooooot · thennnn · Nnnnnno · suuuuper · realllly · Ooooooook · Okayyyyy · nowwwww · shouuuld · thiiiisss · allll · "aaa LOT"

### Scene Transitions
- Annnnd - *"Annnnnnd, now you know when to use recursion!"*
### ALL CAPS = Vocal Stress
Applied to single words mid-sentence
- `BUT`
- `MUST`
- `ONLY`
- `REAL`
- `ACTUALLY`
- `NEEDED`
- `EVERYTHING`
- `IS`
- `ANY`
- `BEST`.

**`BUT!` on its own line** is the standard pivot into a counterpoint.
### Staccato Puntuation
To force word-by-word delivery
> "Stop. Putting. Everything. In. A Goroutine."
> "there are. no. shortcuts."
> "But. still. terrible."
> "keep, taking, every, next, step."

### Ellipsis = A Beat
- *"Go Variables... Are Weirdly Complicated"*
- *"Is the code good… Nnnnnno. it's terrible."* 
- *"Ok…."*
### No Em Dashes
- Zero in any script body
- Use commas, ellipses, or a line break instead

## Rhetorical patterns
### Anit-Dogma is the House Position
- Nearly every opinion video attacks a received best practice: DRY is bad advice, good code is a myth, there are no bad languages, rewrites are always wrong, there are no shortcuts.
- Replace dogma with a concrete rule of thumb: Don’t repeat yourself more than 3 times
### Assert Flat, Soften With Humor
> "There is no such thing as Good Code
> There is definitely Bad code **lol**
> But Good code is a myth"
### Concede The Counterargument Out Loud
- Signposted with `BUT!`, `On the other hand`, `Everything has exceptions`.
### Rhetorical Question → Direct Answer
- *"So why use it at all?"* 
- *"So when should you use recursion?"*
### Own Opinions, Don’t Hide It
- `I think`, `I like`, `for me`, `my cold take`
- Never fake objectivity — but show receipts when there are receipts (the logging video walks through actual benchmarks and eliminates options one at a time on screen).
### Self-Deprecation Humour
> "so I wanted to make a short video that I can reference when I inevitably forget something"
### Meta-Humour About the Format Itself
> "And that concludes my TED talk."
> "don't forget to like and subscribe for more super solid opinions."
> "apparently it helps the channel, which is very new."
> "And subscribe I guess"

### Direct Second Person, Imperative Close
- *"So start expanding your horizons!"*
- *"Define what a win means to you, and push yourself to do it."*
## Do not
- Corporate or marketing polish. No "In this video we will be discussing" (0 occurrences).
- Em dashes.
- "Fixing" the elongated vowels, dropped apostrophes, or staccato periods — they're the delivery notes.
- Fake objectivity or hedging an opinion into mush.
- Hard-cutting to a CTA. It rides inside the flow.
## Quick checklist for a new script
- [ ] Line 1 is the thesis or the strongest claim. No warm-up.
- [ ] Self-intro (and temp, if cold tub) lands *after* the hook.
- [ ] One line per breath; lines end in commas.
- [ ] At least one elongated vowel and one ALL CAPS stress word per section.
- [ ] `Annnd` transitions between sections.
- [ ] A `BUT!` pivot conceding the other side.
- [ ] A concrete rule of thumb, not just a complaint.
- [ ] Stage directions in `**bold**`.
- [ ] Mid-roll CTA woven into a sentence, not bolted on.
- [ ] Closes with: "As always, keep breathing, keep building, and I'll see ya next time, peace out!"
